Privacy Policy

A Network ("we", "our", or "us") operates the A Network application and related services. This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use, store, and disclose information when users access current Web2 mining, wallet continuity, account recovery, Web3 visibility, Web4 settlement preparation, on-chain username identity, EVM peer-to-peer transfers, and Web5 community features.

A Network does not guarantee financial returns. All values, metrics, and any controlled distributions depend on participation, system rules, and network conditions. The platform is designed for ecosystem participation, not speculative investment.

1. Information We Collect

We may collect the following categories of information:

  • Email address and login credentials for account creation, authentication, and account recovery.
  • Email verification metadata, including registration OTP, login OTP, seed-view OTP, PIN reset OTP, and account-restore OTP status, attempt counters, and expiration timestamps.
  • Device, IP, and session identifiers used to secure accounts, apply trusted-device logic, detect abuse, and maintain service integrity.
  • Mining activity data, including session start/completion, elapsed time checks, heartbeat validation, successful session counters, ANTS balances, coin-distribution eligibility, and claim-related audit signals.
  • Wallet addresses, migration wallet addresses, wallet PIN status, encrypted seed vault metadata, or related wallet identifiers when users access Web3 or migration features.
  • Public on-chain data associated with the AnetUsernameRegistry smart contract on BNB Smart Chain (BSC), including the @username you register, the BSC address it resolves to, the reverse-resolved primary name for an address, registration and transfer transaction hashes, and registration fee payments. This data is published on a public blockchain by the user’s own signed transaction and is not private. We do not store your private keys.
  • Support or contact information when users communicate with us.

2. How We Use Information

We use information we collect to:

  • Provide, operate, maintain, and secure the application.
  • Authenticate users, verify email ownership with OTP flows, process one-time account-restore requests, and manage trusted-device sessions.
  • Operate mining features, ANTS-first accounting, referral activation logic, anti-abuse checks, and supply protection controls.
  • Support app features across off-chain Web2 services, Web3 visibility, Web4 settlement preparation, migration wallet handling, and Web5 community metrics.
  • Improve functionality, performance, user experience, and fraud detection.
  • Comply with legal obligations and protect the integrity of the ecosystem.

3. Permissions and Minimum Scope

The current mobile-app release is designed to operate without broad access to sensitive device datasets.

  • Current Android permissions are limited to internet access, local notification support, boot-complete handling for notification scheduling, and advertising identifier support for ad services where permitted.
  • The current release does not request broad contacts access, precise location, background location, geofencing, SMS, call logs, photo-library access, all-files access, or broad package visibility.
  • If a future feature needs contact sharing, we intend to use minimum-scope system flows such as Android Contact Picker where available instead of broad address-book access unless broader access is strictly required for a core, user-facing function.
  • If a future feature needs location, we intend to request the minimum scope needed, such as coarse location, foreground-only access, or Android's location button for one-time precise location flows where available, rather than persistent precise or background access unless strictly required for a core, user-initiated function.

4. Web2, Web3, Web4, and Web5 Context

A Network may include separate but linked product layers:

  • Web2 off-chain features, including app-native account systems, service logic, and ANTS accounting.
  • Web3 visibility features, including wallet displays, migration wallet references, and BEP-20 contract information where technically relevant.
  • Web4 settlement concepts that connect Web2 accounting and ANET-Chain through migration planning and governed product logic.
  • Web5 community features, including the ANTS Program (view-only), colony coordination, and Colony Points (CP) metrics.

Each layer operates independently and must not be understood as overlapping in rewards, payouts, or accounting. Not all app activity is blockchain activity. Some features are off-chain, some are on-chain, and some connect through governed migration or visibility flows.

5. Account Recovery and Wallet Continuity

The current release includes account-continuity and wallet-security features designed to reduce accidental loss without turning the service into a custodial wallet provider.

  • Scheduled account deletion may support a one-time restore flow through email OTP before final deletion completes.
  • Wallet PIN set, change, and reset flows may require email-based verification.
  • Seed phrase access controls may use encrypted server-side seed vault fields, email OTP verification, PIN checks, and audit logging.
  • Legacy local device fallback behavior may exist for users whose older seed backups were not migrated to newer encrypted storage flows.

5a. On-Chain Username Identity (Decentralized Identifier)

A Network operates the AnetUsernameRegistry smart contract on BNB Smart Chain (BSC) at 0x15c848e00610d1bd820b122b81879a66318e1c66. Users may optionally claim a public @username that resolves to their EVM wallet address. The claim transaction is signed locally on the user’s device by the user’s own private key and broadcast to BSC.

  • Public by design. The mapping between @username and EVM address is recorded on the public BSC ledger and is readable by anyone, including third-party explorers such as BscScan. Once published, it cannot be unpublished or hidden.
  • Voluntary. Registering a @username is optional. Users who do not register one continue to use 0x… addresses as before. The 1,000-session Layer 1 eligibility threshold for native ANET conversion is not affected.
  • Fee. Registration costs 0.001 BNB plus standard BSC network gas. The protocol fee is sent on-chain to the A-Network treasury wallet at registration time. Username transfers and primary-name changes may also incur small protocol fees as defined in the contract.
  • No private data on-chain. Only the chosen username string, the address, and operational counters are stored on-chain. No email, password, OTP, PIN, seed phrase, mining record, or other personally identifying account data is written to the registry contract.
  • Reverse resolution. The mobile app may reverse-resolve your EVM wallet address into your primary @username (if any) to display a friendly label in the EVM wallet header, EVM bridge header, send sheet, and account profile dialog. This lookup uses public BSC RPC endpoints.
  • Caching. The app may cache reverse-resolution results in device memory for up to five minutes to reduce RPC traffic. No off-chain copy of the username↔address mapping is retained on our servers as a system of record; the on-chain contract is authoritative.
  • Self-custody. Users are solely responsible for the private key controlling the address that owns their username. Loss of that key may result in permanent loss of control over the username.

5b. EVM Peer-to-Peer Transfers

The EVM wallet inside the app may be used to send and receive BNB, USDT, USDC, ANET (BEP-20), and other supported BEP-20 tokens directly between users on BNB Smart Chain. Recipients may be specified by 0x… address or by registered @username (resolved through the public AnetUsernameRegistry).

  • EVM P2P transfers are executed by the user’s own signed transaction on BSC. We do not custody EVM funds.
  • EVM-side P2P is open to all users and is independent of the 1,000-session gate that applies to native ANET Layer 1 transfers.
  • Standard BSC network gas applies. Additional protocol fees may apply to specific flows (such as bridge swaps via AnetSwap or username registration) and are disclosed in-app before the transaction is signed.
  • On-chain transactions are public. Senders, recipients, amounts, and transaction hashes are visible on BSC explorers.
